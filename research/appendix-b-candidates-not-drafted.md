# Appendix B: Candidates Not Drafted

## Rejected on verification

**AI access audit.** The cited source describes an institutional exercise
mapping which departments and staff have access to which AI tools. Discovery
described an instructor auditing their own course assignments, which is a
different instrument in the same document. As cited it also fails the scope
fence, being institution-level rather than instructor-adoptable.

**AI literacy assignment, as originally cited.** The cited page proved to be a
bibliography of AI literacy frameworks containing no assignment design, and the
named author does not correspond to the actual author. The practice itself
survives as *Assign Critique of AI-Generated Work*, re-grounded on a different
source.

## On hold

**Structured AI draft plus revision assignment.** Cited to a journal article
whose DOI does not resolve in the publisher's index and whose repository record
could not be retrieved. This was the only candidate in the batch claiming
outcome evidence, which makes verification more important rather than less. Not
drafted pending confirmation the source exists as cited.

**Keep humans central.** A value, not an adoptable action. Fails the first unit
criterion.

**Redesign assessments for the AI era.** A chapter-sized bundle of several
practices. Fails the one-decision criterion.

**Integrate AI literacy across the curriculum.** Programmatic rather than
instructor-level. Fails the scope fence.

## Scope boundary case, not drafted

**Shift from few high-stakes assessments to more frequent smaller ones.**
Recommended by a verified source as an AI-era integrity measure, and it passes
every unit criterion. Not drafted because its mechanism contains no AI at all:
it is a grading practice motivated by AI rather than a practice involving it.
This is the first candidate to expose a scope question the unit definition does
not answer, namely whether a playbook is bounded by the mechanism of its plays
or by the problem they address. On the mechanism reading it belongs in the
alternative grading playbook, not this one.

## Rejected on citation grounds, batch two

Six candidates across the three later runs were discarded before assessment
because their citations could not be trusted. Three carried reserved
placeholder domains that cannot exist. Three attributed real documents to
institutions that did not publish them. In each case the underlying practice may
be sound; the citation was unusable and the candidate was not worth the
verification cost. See the batch two citation notes.

## Rejected at discovery

**Offer a choice between AI and non-AI assignments.** Appears as one line inside
a broader syllabus policy rather than as a substantial independent practice.
Folded into *Publish a Transparent Course AI Policy* as an implementation
option.
