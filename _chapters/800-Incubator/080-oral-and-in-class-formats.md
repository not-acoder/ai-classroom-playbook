## Intent

Students demonstrate knowledge in real time rather than exclusively through take-home artifacts.


## Problem

Take-home written work can be produced entirely by a generative model, so it no longer reliably evidences a student's own understanding. Detection tools are unreliable and put the instructor in an adversarial posture toward the class. The alternative is to change what is being assessed rather than trying to police how the artifact was produced.


## Solution

The instructor moves some graded weight to formats requiring live demonstration: oral presentations, group projects, and hands-on activities in which students show their knowledge and skills interactively. These formats make reasoning visible in a way a finished document does not, and they discourage substitution without requiring surveillance.


## Applicability

Works well in:

+ Courses small enough for oral formats to be logistically feasible
+ Courses where skills are demonstrable rather than purely written
+ Project, lab, and studio courses where hands-on demonstration already fits the format

Tradeoffs and limitations:

+ Oral assessment consumes class time and scales badly with enrollment
+ Live formats can disadvantage students with anxiety or students working in a second language unless deliberately designed around that
+ Without a clear rubric, assessment can drift toward rewarding delivery polish over understanding

Not a good fit when:

+ Enrollment makes individual oral assessment impossible without sacrificing instruction
+ The learning outcomes genuinely require extended written work that cannot be demonstrated live


## How to Implement

The source names the formats but does not describe how to adopt them. Steps 1, 2, 3, and 5 are drafted rather than taken from the source.

1. _(Drafted)_ Identify which learning outcomes are currently assessed only through take-home written work.
2. _(Drafted)_ Choose a format that suits the outcome: oral presentation, group project, or hands-on activity.
3. _(Drafted)_ Decide how much of the course grade moves to the live format, and keep the shift partial rather than total.
4. Design the activity so students must demonstrate their knowledge and skills in an interactive way rather than submitting a finished artifact.
5. _(Drafted)_ Build a rubric that assesses reasoning rather than presentation polish, and tell students the change supports authentic demonstration of learning rather than functioning as a punitive measure.
