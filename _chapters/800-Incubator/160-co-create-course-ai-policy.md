## Intent

Students help set the course's AI norms and can articulate the reasoning behind them.


## Problem

An AI policy handed down as a rule invites compliance at best and evasion at worst. Students arrive with more practical experience of these tools than the policy usually reflects, and that experience goes unused. A policy nobody discussed also ages badly, because no one has a reason to revisit it.


## Solution

The instructor treats the policy as a teaching tool rather than as legalese. Permission levels are set through active collaboration with students rather than only by the instructor. The policy is discussed in the first class, the instructor explains the reasoning behind the choices, and it is revisited during the term as a discussion topic rather than a settled matter.


## Applicability

Works well in:

+ Small and medium classes where genuine negotiation is feasible
+ Upper-division and graduate courses where students have professional stakes in the norms
+ Courses running across multiple terms, where the policy can improve iteratively

Tradeoffs and limitations:

+ Students may propose norms the instructor cannot accept, so the boundaries of the negotiation must be clear from the start
+ Consumes class time early in the term
+ Co-created norms can drift from institutional or departmental requirements

Not a good fit when:

+ Institutional or departmental policy is fixed and leaves nothing to negotiate
+ Enrollment makes real deliberation impossible


## How to Implement

The source establishes that permission levels can emerge from collaboration with students and specifies how the policy should be written and discussed, but does not describe the co-creation session itself. Steps 2, 3, and 4 are drafted.

1. Decide the range you are willing to negotiate within. The source notes policy stances can span from allowing AI for all learning tasks with attribution to prohibiting it entirely.
2. _(Drafted)_ Bring a draft policy to class rather than a blank page, so the discussion has something to react to.
3. _(Drafted)_ Have students propose and negotiate specific norms within your stated range.
4. _(Drafted)_ Write the agreed norms up and post them where students will see them.
5. Add the resulting statement to your syllabus and course site, specifying which tools students may use, what uses are allowed, and how students should cite or acknowledge AI use.
6. Discuss the policy during the first class and revisit it throughout the semester, explaining your rationale and making it a discussion topic.
7. Review the statement for clarity, looking for gaps to address and ambiguous wording to revise.
