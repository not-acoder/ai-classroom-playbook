## Intent

The instructor produces a first draft of course or unit learning objectives to revise rather than starting from a blank page.


## Problem

Writing learning objectives is slow, and the difficulty is not conceptual but generative. The instructor knows what students should be able to do and still has to produce objectives that are specific, measurable, and pitched at the right cognitive level, then align them across a unit. The cost falls at the start of course design when time is shortest, so objectives are often carried over unchanged from previous terms or written thinly.


## Solution

The instructor gives the AI the topic, student level, and desired cognitive level, and asks for a set of candidate objectives. The output is treated as a first draft. The instructor revises for alignment with what is actually assessed, discards objectives that do not match the course, and keeps the framing that fits their own teaching.


## Applicability

Works well in:

+ New course design, where nothing exists to revise
+ Course revision where existing objectives are known to be vague
+ Programmes requiring objectives stated at a specified cognitive level

Tradeoffs and limitations:

+ Generated objectives tend toward the generic, and the specificity that makes an objective useful is exactly what the model lacks context for
+ Objectives can drift out of alignment with what the course actually assesses, which is worse than having none
+ Fluent phrasing can disguise an objective that is not measurable

Not a good fit when:

+ Objectives are fixed by accreditation or department and not the instructor's to write


## How to Implement

Steps 2, 4, and 5 are drafted. The sources name learning objectives as a document category generative AI handles adeptly and do not describe a procedure.

1. Ask the AI for a first draft of learning objectives for the topic, using it to get material to work with rather than expecting a finished product.
2. _(Drafted)_ State the student level and the cognitive level you want, since the model will otherwise default to a generic mix.
3. Revise and make the output your own rather than adopting it.
4. _(Drafted)_ Check each objective against what you will actually assess. Delete any objective with no assessment behind it.
5. _(Drafted)_ Check that each objective states something observable. Replace understand and appreciate with verbs you could grade.
