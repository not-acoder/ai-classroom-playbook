## Intent

Project teams agree how AI will be used in their shared work, and that agreement is visible to every member.


## Problem

Course AI policy is written for individuals and group work breaks it. A team member can use a model on the shared deliverable in a way the others neither see nor sanction, and the question of whether that is acceptable surfaces only when someone objects, usually late. Task allocation within teams shifts once AI is available, and the shift happens silently: work that used to be divided and discussed becomes work one person generates alone the night before. The team loses the reason it was a team.


## Solution

The instructor requires each team to produce a short AI use agreement at the start of the project, covering which tasks may be done with AI, whether AI use happens individually or together, and how members tell each other what they used. The agreement is submitted with the project plan. Because AI use in teams tends toward the invisible, the operative provision is usually visibility rather than permission.


## Applicability

Works well in:

+ Semester long team projects, where norms set early hold
+ Courses where the group process is itself assessed
+ Programmes preparing students for collaborative professional work, where the same negotiation happens in industry

Tradeoffs and limitations:

+ Teams will write an agreement that says the right things and then not follow it, unless the agreement is revisited at a checkpoint
+ The evidence base here is thin. Research on AI in student teams is interview based and descriptive, and no study cited establishes that agreements change behaviour
+ A team agreement cannot override course policy and students need telling which governs

Not a good fit when:

+ Group work is brief and low stakes, where the agreement costs more than it saves


## How to Implement

All five steps are drafted. The source characterises how students actually use AI in group projects and does not prescribe an intervention.

1. _(Drafted)_ Require an AI use agreement as part of the project plan, submitted before work starts.
2. _(Drafted)_ Require it to cover three things: which project tasks may involve AI, whether AI use is individual or shared, and how members inform each other of what they used.
3. _(Drafted)_ Default the agreement toward visibility rather than permission. The failure mode in teams is not that a member used AI, it is that nobody else knew.
4. _(Drafted)_ State how the team agreement relates to the course policy, since the team cannot grant permissions the course withholds.
5. _(Drafted)_ Revisit the agreement at a mid project checkpoint, since the first version will have been written before the team knew what the work involved.


## Examples

Tang and colleagues interviewed twenty undergraduate and graduate students across disciplines about AI use in group projects, coding the transcripts against a team AI collaboration framework, and examined usage scenarios, perceived AI roles, and task allocation. The study establishes that AI is reshaping how work is divided within student teams. It does not evaluate any intervention.

The source study for Play 16 supplies an adjacent student generated recommendation: students in that workshop series identified clear assignment specific AI guidelines from instructors as their first policy recommendation, which is the individual level analogue of what this play asks teams to produce for themselves.

This Examples section is thin because the available research characterises the problem rather than documenting a practice that addresses it. See Appendix A.
